Sports Goods Wholesale Buying Checklist: 12 Things to Confirm Before Ordering

A practical buying checklist for importers, distributors and retailers sourcing sports goods from India or other markets.

Published 9 November 2026

A bulk sports-goods order is easier to compare, inspect and receive when the requirements are written down before asking suppliers for prices. Use this checklist to turn a broad buying idea into a quotation-ready brief.

1. Define the product precisely

Name the sport and product, intended use, target customer and market. If you are sourcing more than one category, list each product as a separate line so specifications and prices do not get mixed together.

2. Write the specification

Record material, dimensions, weight, construction, colour, grade, performance requirements and acceptable tolerances where relevant. Attach a drawing, reference photograph or sample if words alone could be interpreted differently.

3. State the quantity by product and variant

Give quantities for each model, size, colour and pack configuration. Ask the supplier to confirm the minimum order quantity for each line rather than assuming one MOQ applies to the entire order.

4. Request a sample and define approval

Ask whether a sample is available, whether it is chargeable, and whether sample and courier costs are refundable or adjustable against a later order. Record what must be approved before bulk production begins.

5. Confirm branding and packaging

Share logo artwork, print or label placement, colour references, retail packaging, barcode and carton-marking requirements. Confirm artwork approval and any setup charges before production.

6. Ask how quality will be checked

Agree the inspection stage, sample size or inspection method, critical defects and how non-conforming goods will be handled. Product-specific checks matter: a ball, racket, net and protective item do not have the same quality criteria.

7. Check compliance for the destination market

Tell the supplier the destination country and intended sales channel. Identify applicable product-safety, labelling, testing or certification requirements before ordering. Do not assume a certificate for one product or market covers another.

8. Compare the full price basis

Ask whether the quotation is ex-works, FOB, CIF or another agreed Incoterm, and specify the named place or port and Incoterms edition. Compare product price, tooling, packaging, inspection, freight, insurance, duties, taxes and local charges—not just the unit price.

9. Confirm production and dispatch timing

Request a written production schedule and clarify when the clock starts, such as after sample approval, artwork approval or receipt of an advance. Allow time for inspection, packing, export clearance and transit.

10. Agree payment milestones and documents

Confirm currency, deposit, balance trigger, accepted payment method and bank details through a verified channel. List the documents required by your customs broker and bank, which may include a commercial invoice, packing list, transport document and certificate of origin where applicable.

11. Plan cartons and shipment handling

Request carton dimensions, gross and net weight, units per carton and total shipment volume. Confirm whether mixed product lines can be consolidated and whether the load is suitable for your freight plan.

12. Put the final agreement in writing

Keep the approved specification, sample reference, price basis, quantities, delivery schedule, inspection terms, packaging artwork and payment milestones together in the purchase order or sales contract. Resolve differences before production.

Send a quotation-ready enquiry

To receive a useful quotation, share the product list, specifications or reference images, quantity by variant, branding and packaging needs, destination country or port, and your target order timeline. If some details are not final, say so and ask which decisions are needed first.

Frequently asked questions

What information should I send for a wholesale quote?

Product name, specification or reference image, quantity by variant, branding and packaging requirements, destination and target timeline are the most useful starting points.

Can I combine different sports products in one order?

Potentially, subject to the products, suppliers, packing requirements and shipment plan. Ask for each line to be quoted separately and confirm consolidation feasibility before placing the order.

Should I order samples before bulk production?

For unfamiliar or customised products, sample approval is a sensible risk-control step. Agree the sample specification and approval criteria in writing.
