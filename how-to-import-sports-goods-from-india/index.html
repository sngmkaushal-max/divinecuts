How to Import Sports Goods from India: A Step-by-Step Guide for Buyers

Samples, minimum quantities, Incoterms, payment terms, inspection and documents — what an importer needs to know before placing a first order in India.

Published 21 September 2026

India is one of the world's main sources of sports equipment, and Punjab has been a centre of the trade for generations. If you are importing for the first time, the process is more predictable than it looks. This guide sets out the steps in the order they usually happen.

1. Define what you actually need

Before contacting suppliers, write down the product, the grade or quality level, quantities per size, any branding, the packaging you want, and the destination port. A clear brief gets you comparable quotations; a vague one gets you quotes you cannot compare.

2. Shortlist manufacturers, not just traders

Ask directly whether the company makes the goods or buys them in. Both can work, but a manufacturer gives you more control over specification and changes. Ask what they produce in-house and what they outsource.

3. Ask for a quotation against your specification

A useful quotation states the unit price, the Incoterm (EXW, FOB or CIF), the minimum order quantity, the lead time, the packing, and how long the price is valid. If any of these are missing, ask before comparing prices, because an FOB price and a CIF price are not the same thing.

4. Approve a pre-production sample

Never skip this. A sample turns a written specification into a physical reference. Approve it in writing, keep one yourself, and make it the standard the bulk order is checked against. Sample charges are common and are often adjusted against the first order.

5. Agree payment terms

New relationships commonly use an advance with the balance against shipping documents, or a letter of credit for larger orders. Discuss this early: terms affect price, and no reputable supplier will be offended by the question.

6. Plan branding and packaging

If you are buying under your own brand, send artwork in a printable format and confirm where each logo, label and sticker goes. Decide on retail packaging, inner and master cartons, carton markings and barcodes at this stage, not after production.

7. Arrange inspection

You can inspect yourself, appoint a third-party agency, or rely on the supplier's own checks against the approved sample. Whichever you choose, agree it before production starts and state what will be checked.

8. Confirm documents and shipping

Standard export documents include the commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or airway bill and certificate of origin. Your customs broker will tell you what else your country requires, including any duty, labelling or safety rules that apply to sports goods.

9. Start smaller than you think

A trial order tests quality, packing, timing and communication at low risk. Most long-term supply relationships begin this way.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum order quantity for sports goods from India?

It varies by product and by whether the goods are standard or custom. Custom and branded items usually carry a higher minimum than standard lines, because tooling, artwork and materials are set up for your order.

How long does production take?

Lead time depends on the product, the quantity and whether branding is involved. Ask for the lead time in writing with the quotation, measured from approval of the sample and receipt of advance payment.

Should I buy FOB or CIF?

FOB gives you control of freight and insurance through your own forwarder, which experienced importers often prefer. CIF is simpler for a first order because the supplier arranges shipping to your port.

Can I import under my own brand?

Yes. Private-label and OEM production is standard practice. Send your artwork and packaging requirements with the enquiry so they are quoted from the start.
